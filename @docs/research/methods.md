# Methods: What Works

Date: 2026-10-20. Links checked via search, full texts not opened; figures are from abstracts. Without a link — from general knowledge (Swain, Nation, Schmidt, Bjork), not verified. No meta-analyses found on translation as a method or on dictation.

## Ranking
1. **Retrieval from memory + spaced repetition (FSRS).** L2 meta-analysis (48 experiments, N=3411): medium/large effect, long intervals win on delayed tests ([Kim](https://ir.lib.uwo.ca/etd/8600/)). FSRS is a scheduler, not a method ([open-spaced-repetition](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition)). Cards only in context (cloze on a sentence), not "word–translation".
2. **Output (pushed output) + error feedback.** Written correction gives a stable moderate effect ([Brown, Liu & Norouzian 2023](https://todoele.net/node/271041), [Kang & Han 2015](https://scinapse.io/papers/2005247852)). RU↔EN translation with immediate comparison to a reference is the main block. No direct meta-analyses on translation.
3. **Listening to varied voices (HVPT).** g=0.92 pre/post, 0.67 vs. control, 79 studies ([Uchihara et al. 2025](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/high-variability-phonetic-training-hvpt-a-metaanalysis-of-l2-perceptual-training-studies/6ABB8C1F32D88D53EA8D05A4565E76F6/core-reader)). Many accents, not one speaker. Plus dictation: write it down, check, go through what was missed.
4. **Shadowing.** Helps intelligibility and prosody; effect on individual sounds unclear ([Oxford, 44 studies](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:3104cae1-3a6b-400a-b173-de44384238d2)). 5–8 minutes.
5. **Generated/graded stories (i+1).** 95–98% known words in the text. A story of ~200 words fits.
6. **Interleaving and difficulty adaptation.** Target ~80–85% correct answers (Bjork). A heuristic; no L2 meta-analyses.

## What Not to Do
- Gamification (you dislike it, link to results is weak).
- Single "word–translation" cards.
- Passive videos without a check at 60–80% comprehension.
- More than 5–8 new words a day.
- Only heavy translation with a dictionary: take paragraphs of 3–5 sentences and compare with the reference right away.
- Correcting everything: 1–2 categories per session.

## 50-Minute Cycle
| Block | Min | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1. FSRS reviews | 10 | Cloze from words and past errors, answer aloud |
| 2. Listening + dictation | 12 | 20–40 s excerpt, a different accent each time |
| 3. Shadowing | 6 | Same excerpt, then without text |
| 4. Translation (main) | 15 | RU→EN paragraph from a story on your words, review discrepancies; every 2–3 days EN→RU |
| 5. Consolidation | 7 | Block 4 errors → cards, short work correspondence |

90 minutes: add extensive listening 20–30 min (different accents, no stops). 30 minutes: blocks 1, 4, 5.

## Error Tracking
Record: category, source text, correction, date, block. Categories: vocabulary, tenses/conjugation, articles, prepositions, word order, collocations, register, listening (with accent noted).
1. Count per category over 7 days: a growing category gets 60% of the time in block 4.
2. Each error → FSRS card (cloze on the correct form).
3. Repeated error → frequent repeats + different context.
4. Listening stats separately by accent.
5. Every 2 weeks, a control task without hints.
6. Errors with no repeats for 4 weeks leave the active sample.

## Hooks (association, keyword method)
Added 2026-10-20 after a ChatGPT session. One search, no meta-analysis found; primary studies only, texts not opened.
- Keyword-style mnemonics help most at low proficiency and for first recall; the gain can fade faster than rote rehearsal when the word is not retrieved again ([UCF abstract](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/facultybib1990/1773)). Combined with retrieval practice it helps the receptive side ([2023 EFL study](https://doaj.org/article/035cb63742544fc19fcd8228faec5938)).
- Rule taken: a hook is one short line, supported by a return in a new sentence (the example card), never a replacement for retrieval. Test first, reveal the hook after the attempt.
- Hooks that build on known English (`insufficient` is not `sufficient`), contrast near words (`plausible` vs `possible`) or keep the whole chunk (`draw a conclusion`) fit a B1/B2 learner better than invented sound-alike images. A hook that is only a mnemonic must be labelled so, not passed off as etymology.

## Habits: tracking recurring errors
Added 2026-10-21. One search; the closest source is one small study (5 learners), so the thresholds below are our choice, not a finding.
- Error analysis separates slips (tired, accidental) from errors that repeat from a missing rule ([handout](https://sbrc2017.ufpa.br/trip/error-analysis-and-interlanguage)). A defence-language programme coded an error as a recurring "key form" only on set criteria, and with error-pattern detection plus spaced review the key-form errors fell about 40% over 19 weeks ([DLIFLC](https://www.dliflc.edu/ojs/ALL/article/download/69/61/172)). Error charting (a running log of recurring grammar errors) is a standard classroom tool ([JALT](https://jalt-publications.org/articles/26266-error-charting-during-novel-times)).
- No source gave an "error resolved" criterion. Ours: a rule is `watch` after one slip, `active` after two misses, `improving` after 2 correct uses since the last miss, `resolved` after 4 correct uses and 14 quiet days; a new miss reopens it. Counting correct uses needs the opportunity to be recorded, so the tutor lists the rules a sentence needed and got right (`patterns_held`).
